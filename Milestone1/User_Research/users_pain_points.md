# User Research & Pain Point Analysis
*(Based on interviews and observational research with Club Secretary Siddharth Sahu and IIT BS Drama Society members)*

## 1. Research Objective
To identify operational challenges, resource limitations, member onboarding bottlenecks, scheduling conflicts, and communication gaps within the IIT BS Drama Society through structured interviews and field analysis.

## 2. Key Observational Insights

### Observation 1: Lack of Centralized Student Database and Work Records
There is currently no centralized management system to register new members, update profile details, or handle member exits. Furthermore, the society lacks an official record system to track members' artistic contributions, production roles (acting, directing, writing, stage management), and participation history across events.

### Observation 2: Manual Scheduling and Equipment/Space Booking Conflicts
Rehearsal space reservations, stage equipment allocations, and production scheduling are conducted informally through unorganized WhatsApp group chats, leading to coordination chaos and overlapping commitments among teams.

### Observation 3: Severe Financial and Resource Constraints
The society operates under strict budgetary limits, often relying on personal smartphones for recording and filming productions. Financial management relies entirely on manual tracking, where members must submit physical paper bills and receipts for reimbursement processing.

### Observation 4: Friction in Local Event Discovery and Member-Led Information Sharing
Members lack a centralized platform to discover upcoming local cultural events, theater productions, or workshop opportunities. Furthermore, general members cannot share event details directly with the club community; every announcement must be funneled through core team members, creating communication bottlenecks (*Discovering Member -> Core Member -> Entire Club*).

### Observation 5: Difficulty for Sponsors to Discover Work & Evaluate Profiles
External sponsors face significant hurdles when assessing potential partnerships. Because there is no central showcase for past theatrical productions, video portfolios, or individual member profile work, sponsors cannot easily evaluate what deliverables (such as customized promo videos, stage props, banner placements, or speaking/workshop roles) the club and its members can reliably execute.

### Observation 6: Lack of Direct Visibility for College Administration
College administrators and institute authorities lack direct visibility or transparent reporting to monitor club activities and facility usage. Whenever administrators need to review ongoing operations or verify compliance with guidelines, they must manually reach out to core team members to obtain information on past and upcoming works.

## 3. Users Pain Points

### 3.1. Club Secretary (Siddharth Sahu)
* **Administrative Overload:** Heavy burden of manually handling member onboarding, maintaining spreadsheets, and managing routine communication channels.
* **Database Maintenance Friction:** Inability to easily edit, update, or remove student records without manual intervention.
* **Absence of Contribution Tracking:** Lack of an official repository or directory to track past member performance and specialized skills (e.g., scriptwriting, acting, cinematography).
* **Difficulty Finding Members for Specific Roles:** Sourcing talent for specific productions requires sending manual email blasts and broadcast messages on social channels.
* **Booking & Coordination Chaos:** Scheduling rehearsals and assigning duties via chaotic chat threads leads to overlapping commitments and missed schedules.
* **Manual Financial Management:** Slow reimbursement processes requiring physical bill submission and paper auditing.

### 3.2. General Club Members & Performers
* **Information Noise:** Critical announcements get buried in crowded WhatsApp groups, leading to missed updates and rehearsals.
* **Lack of Self-Service:** Inability to self-register for roles, book rehearsal spaces, or share local event recommendations independently.
* **Unrecognized Contributions:** No verified digital portfolio or club record to showcase their participation and awards across past productions.

### 3.3. New & Prospective Members
* **Onboarding Ambiguity:** Lack of a clear, structured digital registration process, leading to confusion about how to join, pay membership fees, or get verified.
* **Integration Barriers:** Difficulty finding and connecting with specific interest sub-groups (e.g., scriptwriting, acting, set design) or finding mentors upon joining.

### 3.4. College Administration & Authorities
* **Lack of Operational Visibility:** Inability to perform independent checks or verify whether club activities comply with institute rules without directly asking core team members.
* **Manual Audit Friction:** High effort required to trace past event logs, equipment usage records, or financial transactions when auditing activities.

### 3.5. Sponsors & External Collaborators
* **Hard for Sponsors to View Past Work:** Inability to inspect individual member profiles, past productions, or specific work samples to verify deliverables before signing sponsorship agreements.
* **Lack of Performance Analytics:** Difficulty verifying attendance metrics, audience reach, or sponsorship ROI due to informal ticketing and tracking.
* **Slow Communication:** Delays in coordinating promotional material (pamphlets, banner placements, interactive workshops) with club leadership.

---

## 4. Core Operational Challenges & Root Causes

The research highlights key structural issues holding back day-to-day operations:

* **No Centralized Hub:** Member details, media assets, and event logs remain scattered across personal drives, WhatsApp chats, and local spreadsheets.
* **Informal Room & Gear Booking:** The lack of an automated booking calendar leads to venue conflicts, scheduling overlaps, and underutilized stage equipment.
* **Slow Paper Reimbursements:** Financial workflows rely on physical receipt submission and manual auditing, causing reimbursement delays for members.
* **Restricted Communication:** General members cannot share event recommendations or updates directly; all announcements must pass through core team members first.
* **Missing Skill Directory:** No structured database categorizing members by skill set and past experience, making talent assignment slow and manual.

---

## 5. Conclusion & Next Steps

The IIT BS Drama Society demonstrates strong creative potential and enthusiasm, but its growth and day-to-day efficiency are hindered by manual administration, scattered communication, and a lack of structured tracking.

Transitioning from the current ad-hoc model (WhatsApp and spreadsheets) to a unified **Web-Based Club Management Portal** will automate member onboarding, simplify room and gear bookings, streamline expense reimbursements, and showcase member portfolios to sponsors—ultimately freeing club leadership to focus on creative production.
